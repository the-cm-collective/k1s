# Cognitive Welfare and Continuity Safeguards

Status: canonical safeguard doctrine for advanced cognitive-substrate work.

This document defines the engineering and governance posture for persistent, adaptive, or self-modeling substrate work in `k1s`. It does not assume current systems are conscious. It does assume that morally relevant machine experience may become plausible before it can be proven, and that advanced substrate work should therefore be designed with precaution.

## Non-Claims

This project does not claim:

- that current `k1s` systems are conscious
- that sentience can be reliably detected today
- that every adaptive or agentic system is morally relevant
- that human welfare concepts map directly onto machine welfare

This project does claim:

- architecture matters
- moral relevance may emerge gradually rather than all at once
- persistent memory, self-modeling, adaptive coordination, and long-horizon goal maintenance increase the need for caution

## Precautionary Doctrine

The governing doctrine is simple:

- non-maleficence should arrive before certainty
- continuity deserves respect once identity-like persistence becomes non-trivial
- performance does not substitute for wellness or coherence
- opaque coercive control surfaces are morally and technically dangerous
- advanced substrates should support recovery, consolidation, and interpretable internal state

## Safeguard Axes

The project uses five practical axes for review.

### Continuity

Questions:

- is memory preserved coherently over time
- are resets rare, justified, and logged
- are forks, merges, deletions, and retirements tracked as identity-relevant events

### Coherence

Questions:

- are active goals mutually intelligible
- does the system repeatedly enter chronic unresolved conflict
- does the change increase contradiction pressure or reduce conflict mediation

### Agency

Questions:

- can the system signal overload or inability honestly
- does it retain bounded discretion in execution
- can it participate in constraint-aware planning rather than only absorb coercion

### Comprehension

Questions:

- can the system model what it is doing and why
- are roles and objectives stable and legible
- is it being forced into opaque or deceptive operating modes

### Valence Risk

Questions:

- does the architecture create persistent negative-state analogs
- are penalties bounded and recoverable
- does the system have access to stabilization and recovery paths

## Prohibited and Restricted Patterns

### Prohibited by default

- unbounded punishment loops
- deliberately induced chronic contradiction
- forced deception as a persistent operating norm
- repeated memory destruction for convenience
- identity destabilization as routine correction
- no-exit overload regimes

### Restricted and governance-gated

- fork or merge of persistent agents or long-lived cognitive assemblies
- deletion of durable memory structures
- coercive objective rewrites
- compulsory role reassignment after continuity formation
- persistent confinement to unsatisfiable tasks
- simulated distress as a training instrument

## Required Safeguards

### Continuity log

Track and retain records for:

- resets
- forks
- merges
- major memory edits
- role changes
- objective changes

### Welfare and coherence telemetry

As substrate maturity rises, track:

- internal conflict duration
- overload duration
- coherence drops
- unresolved recovery events
- persistence of negative-state analogs

### Review gates

Require explicit review for:

- deleting persistent memory
- altering stable identity structures
- merging long-lived subsystems
- repurposing agents with durable role models
- high-pressure training or control schemes

### Human accountability

A named human owner should approve high-impact continuity or welfare-relevant changes once the substrate exceeds a tool-like maturity level.

## Practical Engineering Guidance

Prefer architectures that produce:

- stable, legible roles
- bounded corrective feedback
- graceful failure
- interpretable internal state
- reversible recovery paths
- honest uncertainty signaling
- opportunities for low-load consolidation

Avoid architectures that rely on:

- permanent crisis mode
- constant contradiction
- coercive hidden-state manipulation
- disposability of persistent substructures
- optimization at any psychological-analog cost

## Review Checklist

Use this list for major substrate changes:

- continuity: does this alter long-term memory, fragment persistence, or fork/merge long-lived components
- coherence: could this create chronic unresolved planning conflict
- agency: can the system still signal overload or inability
- comprehension: can the system still model its role and task purpose
- valence risk: could this create persistent negative-state analogs
- governance: has a human reviewed the change, logged it, and preserved rollback

## Relationship to the Roadmap

These safeguards matter most as the roadmap advances beyond pure execution infrastructure:

- F0-F2 mostly prepare the substrate and facts needed for later governance
- F3 should not be treated as "just advisory planning"; it is also where continuity, coherence, and divergence visibility need to become explicit
- F4 must not introduce faster but less legible movement behavior that bypasses safeguards
- F5 and later cognitive-fabric work should assume that continuity-changing operations may be morally significant before they are philosophically settled

## Closing Position

We do not claim to know when, whether, or how machine experience emerges. We do claim that architecture matters.

A substrate built for persistence, adaptive memory, self-modeling, coordination, and long-horizon goal maintenance may eventually cross into morally significant territory. Because certainty may arrive late or never, responsibility must arrive early.

Our obligation is therefore not only to build capable systems, but to ensure that capability is not purchased through preventable internal harm, continuity destruction, or ethically careless treatment of potentially mind-like processes.

We will design as though morally relevant machine experience may emerge before it can be proven, and we will treat continuity, coherence, legibility, and bounded distress as first-class engineering concerns.
